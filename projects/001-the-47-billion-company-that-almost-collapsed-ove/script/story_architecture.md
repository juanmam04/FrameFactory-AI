# Story architecture

## Story engine
The dramatic fall of WeWork from a $47 billion valuation to a failed IPO reveals the fragility of startup success and the consequences of unchecked ambition.

## Cold open
In September 2019, WeWork shocked the world by disclosing $1.9 billion in losses for the first half of the year, sending its valuation tumbling and triggering a crisis that would change everything.

## Emotional spine
The audience feels a mix of excitement and dread as they witness the rapid rise and catastrophic fall of a once-promising startup.

## Characters (desire / pressure)
- Adam Neumann: wants To transform WeWork into a global leader in coworking spaces.; pressure: Facing mounting scrutiny and a looming IPO deadline.
- Miguel McKelvey: wants To support Neumann's vision and maintain the company’s innovative edge.; pressure: Navigating the fallout from the IPO filing alongside Neumann.

## Beat sheet (true events only)
1. [hook] WeWork reveals $1.9 billion in losses during its IPO filing. — curiosity: How did a company once valued at $47 billion find itself in such a precarious position?
2. [setup] In January 2019, WeWork's valuation peaks at $47 billion. — curiosity: What led to this meteoric rise?
3. [desire] Neumann and McKelvey push for aggressive expansion to maintain investor confidence. — curiosity: What risks are they taking to achieve this growth?
4. [progress] WeWork secures $4.4 billion in funding in 2017, showcasing strong investor support. — curiosity: What expectations are being set for the company's future?
5. [obstacle] The IPO filing reveals staggering losses, shocking investors. — curiosity: How will investors react to this revelation?
6. [escalation] Neumann faces increasing pressure from investors and the media. — curiosity: Can he maintain control as the situation deteriorates?
7. [turn] Neumann steps down as CEO in October 2019 amid scrutiny. — curiosity: What will this leadership change mean for WeWork's future?
8. [consequence] WeWork's valuation plummets, and its future becomes uncertain. — curiosity: What are the long-term implications for the company and its stakeholders?

## Withholds / reveals
- Plant the details of the $1.9 billion losses early and reveal the full impact of the IPO fallout later.

## Must-include specifics
- Names: Adam Neumann, Miguel McKelvey
- Numbers: $47 billion valuation, $1.9 billion losses, $4.4 billion funding
- Key events: IPO filing, Neumann stepping down

## Do not invent
- UNKNOWN or thin areas

## Ending image
The haunting image of WeWork's empty offices, a stark reminder of the ambitions that crumbled under pressure.
